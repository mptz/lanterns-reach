# Reviewer notes for step 2 (full records)

Cross-region notes from the nine region reviewers. Structural fixes are applied; the lore and range notes below are for the record-filling step.

## From the europe-mediterranean reviewer
- **middle-east `cedar-of-lebanon`:** it lists "Atlas cedar" as a name, which duplicates europe's atlas-cedar entry. Remove the name.
- **middle-east `turkish-pine`:** tier 3 looks too low for a major forest tree of Turkey, Cyprus and Crete. I suggest tier 2.
- **north-america:** no entry covers green ash, sweet birch, red spruce (the Adirondack tonewood), hawthorns, common hackberry, crab apples (*Malus coronaria*, *M. fusca*), American hazel, American hornbeam, American rowan, eastern wahoo or the American elders. Europe's entries currently stand in for them. Consider giving the bigger ones their own entries.
- **east-asia:** bitter or Seville orange (*Citrus × aurantium*: neroli, marmalade, Seville's street trees) and bergamot are missing from every file. They are Asian in origin but Mediterranean in culture.


## From the north-america reviewer
- **europe-mediterranean:**
  - `norway-spruce` lists "white spruce" and "black spruce" in its names and *Picea rubens* in `stands_for`; all three are North American, and red spruce is now its own North America entry.
  - `birch` stands for *Betula lenta* and names "paper birch". Sweet birch is now in North America under yellow-birch, and paper birch has its own entry.
  - `mediterranean-hackberry` absorbs *Celtis occidentalis* and `hornbeam` absorbs *Carpinus caroliniana*. Both are North American; acceptable as near relatives, but they would sit better under North America.
  - `crab-apple` absorbs the North American *Malus coronaria* and *M. fusca*.
- **east-asia:** has no mizunara (*Quercus crispula*, whisky casks) and no *Tsuga diversifolia*. Chinese witch hazel (*Hamamelis mollis*) belongs there too.
- **middle-east-central-asia:** Greek juniper (*Juniperus excelsa*) appears in no region.
- **central-south-america:** check that red mangrove and the south Florida hammock trees (gumbo limbo, West Indian mahogany, royal palm) note their Florida range. Montezuma cypress reaches the Rio Grande in Texas.

## From the sub-saharan-africa reviewer
- **middle-east-central-asia `african-frankincense`** (*Boswellia papyrifera*) duplicates `ethiopian-frankincense` here. Its native range is Horn of Africa and Sahel, so I'd remove it there.
- **middle-east `shittim-acacia`** has *V. tortilis* subsp. *raddiana* in `stands_for`, which overlaps `umbrella-thorn` here. I'd remove raddiana there. Also, *V. seyal* is mainly a Sahel and East African tree.
- **central-south-america `kapok`** (*Ceiba pentandra*) is also native to West Africa. Add the African lore there: the Freetown Cotton Tree (fell in 2023) and Equatorial Guinea's coat of arms.
- **central-south-america `red-mangrove` and `black-mangrove`** also occur in West Africa. *Rhizophora mucronata*, the East African and Indo-Pacific mangrove, has no entry anywhere; south-southeast-asia should add it.
- **europe-mediterranean `dragon-tree`:** the Cape Verde subspecies, *D. draco* subsp. *caboverdeana*, is Cape Verde's national tree. Add that lore there.
- **central-south-america** could add *Elaeis oleifera* (American oil palm) if it wants that tree.
